A routine ear-tip change left my earbuds unusable.

01WHAT HAPPENED

I tried to replace a part Bose describes as removable.

For the first time since buying these Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds, I tried to change the silicone ear tips. This should be routine maintenance: Bose sells replacement fit kits and publishes instructions for removing and replacing the tips.

But when I removed the silicone, a hard component came away with it. The electronics were exposed and the part would not click back into place. The only improvised option appeared to be glue—hardly an acceptable repair for a premium electronic product.

04WHY THIS MATTERS

A replaceable tip should not become a replacement device.

Europe and France are moving toward longer-lasting, more repairable products. The EU's 2024 Right to Repair Directive requires national application from 31 July 2026, but its manufacturer repair obligation applies only to product categories already covered by EU repairability requirements. France's current repairability index also covers selected categories—not earbuds. So these rules do not automatically prove a legal right to repair in this case. They do, however, make the policy direction clear: repair should be easier, products should last longer, and premature electronic waste should be avoided.
